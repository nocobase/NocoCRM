# The demo dataset

Read this before changing the demo dataset, its content files, its mail, its WhatsApp conversations, its import and removal, or the demo banner.

The demo dataset is a product feature (`server/providers/crm/demo.ts`, imported and removed from the settings page), not a script or a seed. The settings' Demo data tab (`/settings/demo-data`, `client/pages/settings/demo-data.tsx`) reads the dataset's state from its query and tells the outcome in a toast, since importing or removing it changes the importer's roles and the route guards mount the page anew while the new permissions load; the outcome is written at once into that query and into the workspace's `demoPresent`, which the banner reads, and an import whose request a gateway cuts off (a 502, 503 or 504, or a lost connection, as a hosted deployment's proxy gives up before the server finishes) is not taken for a failure: the card asks how the dataset stands every three seconds, for up to three minutes, until it is there. It comes in Chinese and English, chosen on import (`language` on `POST /demoData/import`, recorded in the manifest): the same story, told with people and companies of that language: its text and everything derived from a name (the demo accounts, each language's own, looked up by the part they play, `DemoSeat`; mailboxes; website and email domains) in `server/providers/crm/demo-content/` (`zh-CN.ts`, `en-US.ts`, one `DemoContent` shape), and everything else shared in `demo.ts`, which holds no text of its own. Change both content files together. Its texts are long on purpose, so the pages show how long text reads: companies' introductions (which their deals' descriptions reuse) and leads' original messages run to several paragraphs, as do the leads' company introductions and every street address in the particulars, the tasks' description, results and the approvals; each of the eight companies has meeting notes on its timeline, some answered by a colleague (`companyNotes`); every converted lead carries the tasks done on it before its conversion and every invalid one the first contact that set it aside, each with its result (`convertedLeadTasks`, `invalidLeadTasks`, H World's `leadTask`); H World's lead is converted with its deal, so its earlier task links the lead, the company and the deal at once; the importer takes part in some of the colleagues' deal tasks, ahead and past (`internalParticipants`); every task done or cancelled is written as it happened, created open and then closed at its time with its result (the import's `saver`), so its timeline reads created, then completed with the result under that entry; and a comment's heading is a plain line above its text, never bold, a comment being what a person writes. The facts in the texts agree with the records' fields (employees, city, amounts). The administrator importing it is the demo's main account, a sales manager for its lifetime: they decide the sellers' and the manager's approvals (two waiting, one decided), and own, besides a share of the team's records, four projects at real companies (`projects-zh-CN.ts` and `projects-en-US.ts`, the same companies in both, their people and exchanges the demo's own): SF Express's new lead, H World's converted lead and deal at proposal with a discount out to the manager and tomorrow's review, GAC's deal in negotiation with approved payment terms and an overdue task, and New Hope won with an extension. The AI's work on them is written as its cards keep it (`crmHistory` rows of action `ai`, results typed against `Verdict`, `OpportunityVerdict`, `TaskPreparation` and `ApprovalReview`), so the import needs no model and the importer's inbox opens on five suggestions waiting and four handled; as the import ends the manager @-mentions them, replies to their comment and gives them a task, which the notification pass delivers. The importer also finds four finished conversations with Noco about those projects under the menu's Conversations (`chats` in the projects files): the Agents plugin creates each conversation (it opens none without a chat model, so a deployment without one gets the conversation row written as the plugin writes it, with the assistant's configuration from its configurator and the placeholder model of `crm-agents/placeholder-model.ts`; `tests/logic/demo-conversations.test.ts`) and its messages are written into the plugin's `agConversationMessages` as its store writes them, the first question carrying the record it was asked about as its page context (H World's and GAC's deals, SF Express's lead), so the bar names it (`demoChats` in `server/routes/demo-services.ts`, `@temporary(nocobase-official)` until the plugin can import a finished conversation), recorded in the manifest as `chats` and deleted with the demo data; none is made without the plugin or before Noco exists. Removing the demo data (`resetDemo`) also deletes from everyone's inbox, once its records are gone, the in-app notifications whose route target names a CRM record that no longer exists, its own and any an earlier removal or a deletion left (`DemoInbox.removeOrphans` in `demo-services.ts`: the plugin's items read and checked against the CRM's tables, a former `/crm/accounts/` path against the companies', deleted through its `inAppNotificationStoreToken` store), and disables the demo accounts an import created (those of either language with a demo username at `@demo.example`; an existing account an import reused stays as it was), and importing again enables them and resets their passwords: they are disabled rather than deleted because the users plugin keeps a deleted account's address and username, so a deleted demo company could not be created again.

The import also sets the business settings the dataset is written for (`demoSettings` in `demo.ts`): the currency to CNY, since every amount in it is in yuan; the business time zone to Asia/Shanghai for Chinese and America/New_York for English; and the AI working language to the dataset's. It sets only a setting still at its default (`CrmService.defaults`: `defaultSettings`, with the currency, the business time zone and the AI working language following `i18n.defaultLocale` — CNY for Simplified Chinese, JPY for Japanese, EUR for German, French and Spanish, USD otherwise (`defaultCurrencyFor`); the language's country's zone, US Eastern for English and any other (`defaultTimeZoneFor`, both in `schema.ts`, since 2026-10-22)), so an administrator's own choice stands, and does so before anything is dated, since the dates are laid out from today in that zone. The keys it set are recorded as `settings` in the manifest and reported by `GET /demoData`, and the Demo data tab names them while the dataset is there; a failed import puts them back, a removal leaves them. Scheduled task titles are written in the AI language at boot, so they follow a change only after a restart.

While no demo dataset is imported, the application layout shows whoever may import one (the settings capability, told by `demoPresent` in the metadata) a banner under the header (`DemoBanner` in `client/components/crm/demo-banner.tsx`, an application change to `client/layouts/app-layout.tsx`) linking to the settings' Demo data tab (`/settings/demo-data`); it goes once the import is done, and dismissing it is remembered in the browser (`localStorage`). While an import runs, dismissed or not, it shows how far it has got instead, on the registry `Progress` primitive: `GET /demoData` reports `importing` with the percent the import this process runs has reached (checkpoints in `populate`, the mailboxes, which take longest, step by step; null when another process runs it, the bar then indeterminate), the banner asks every second while it does, and the settings card marks the import under way the moment it starts it; `demoPresent` is false while the marker is only an import's claim, and a claim ten minutes past its last checkpoint that no process holds (a server stopped mid-import, a dev server restarting on a file change among them) counts as none, a new import taking back what the claim recorded (each checkpoint keeps the manifest so far in it, `progress` in `populate`) and claiming it again (`abandoned` in `demo.ts`).

The demo dataset brings mail of its own, so the mailbox, the records' Mail tabs, the timeline and the follow-ups can be tried without connecting a real mailbox. `server/providers/crm/demo-mail.ts` is a mail provider of the CRM's own, type and instance `crm-demo` (configured in the defaults of `server/config/mail.ts`; a deployment's `mail.providers.crm-demo.enabled: false` turns it and the demo mailboxes off), registered at boot through the Mail plugin's public `mailProviderRegistryToken` and labelled in the demo's language (`server/locales/crm-ai/demo-mail.ts`). The import writes about sixty conversations into the `crmSettings` row `demo-mail`: a thread for every open deal (two for the busiest), the won and lost deals around their closing, the working, nurtured and invalid leads (one bounced by the delivery system, `postmaster`), exchanges between colleagues, and in every mailbox a newsletter, an event invitation, release notes and an office notice that concern no record (`everyday` in `demo.ts`; the topics' text in `demo-content/mail-zh-CN.ts` and `mail-en-US.ts`, the same topics in both, with placeholders for the dataset's people, and their senders as `mailSenders`). `buildDemoMail` files them in Inbox, Sent and Trash, threaded by their headers, the last message received unread, an exchange between colleagues in both mailboxes, no customer's message newer than two days, so nothing starts a follow-up, and no mail with a new lead, which would turn it working, then connects a mailbox `<username>@demo.example` for the importer and each demo seat through `MailService.connectAccount` with a password only this process holds (`DEMO_MAIL_SECRET`), recorded as `mailAccounts` in the manifest; removing the demo data removes those mailboxes and the row. The plugin copies a mailbox's messages once, on its first sync, and keeps read, starred and moved state itself, so the provider reports no changes; a message sent from a demo mailbox is accepted and filed in Sent without being delivered, and one to any address outside the `.example` domains is refused. Since a newly connected mailbox's history arrives dated before the timeline's cursor, `MailTimeline` keeps per user the mailboxes whose first sync it has seen (`synced`, from `listSyncRuns`) and reads the whole 180 days again when one appears.

The demo dataset brings two WhatsApp numbers too (`server/providers/crm/demo-whatsapp.ts`), so the WhatsApp page with its number filter, the records' WhatsApp tabs and their timeline lines can be tried without a Meta account. The import writes two `crmWaNumbers` rows of mode `demo` (`demoNumbers`: the sales line `+1 555 010 0199` and the line abroad `+44 7700 900123`, both from ranges kept for fiction, their business names the content's `displayNames`) and eight conversations as the webhook would have kept them (`demoWhatsAppSchedule`, each naming its number, the texts in `demo-content/whatsapp-zh-CN.ts` and `whatsapp-en-US.ts`, message for message; `tests/logic/demo-whatsapp.test.ts` checks they agree): on the sales line Shanghai Electric's and United Family Healthcare's decision makers, the working lead from Tongwei, and H World's IT director and its evaluator (Cheng Hao, 16 messages on the POC's tests), so H World's company and deal hold two people's messages, linked by their numbers (`+86` and the record's phone), with a line a stretch of conversation on the records they reach (`WhatsAppService.rewriteTimeline`), and a job seeker set aside; on the line abroad a stranger known by a username alone and an integrator, nobody's yet. H World's deal's waiting follow-up is one a customer's WhatsApp reply started (`customerReplied`, last night just after Chen Lu's messages), its brief, prep and reasons drawn from them (the SLA he asked for, the remote presentation, procurement comparing prices), so the AI's use of WhatsApp shows on a card. Four customers' messages carry a picture or a file, their text the caption (`media` in the schedule, naming the content's `files`): Shanghai Electric's photo of its paper inspection sheet and its production reports' sample (a CSV) before the trial import, H World's load test report as a screenshot, and the job seeker's CV (a PDF). Each language has its own (`demo-content/whatsapp-files-zh-CN.ts`, `whatsapp-files-en-US.ts`), the bytes kept there as base64 so the import needs no network, generated with headless Chrome from HTML with made-up people and figures; the import stores them with the CRM's files as a downloaded one is (`WhatsAppService.storeFile`), and the file sweep removes them once a removal has deleted their messages. Three conversations are long, so the page's paging and a record's long history can be tried: Shanghai Electric's (55 messages over six weeks, past the 50 a conversation first shows, so Load earlier appears), H World's (33 over a month, the POC to the quote) and the integrator's (24 over two weeks); the others are a few messages each. The team's replies are origin `businessApp`, as made in the WhatsApp Business app, since the CRM does not send yet. A conversation awaits a reply as the webhook would count it (`awaitingReply`: the customer's messages since the team's last reply), five of them, nine messages in all; demo numbers are not `api`, so opening one does not clear it. No message is newer than yesterday and none sets `followUpDueAt`, so nothing starts a follow-up. The numbers are recorded as `whatsapp` in the manifest (version 8, `numberIds`; an import from before the second number has `numberId`, which removal still reads) before anything is written; removal deletes them with their conversations, messages, customers and their lines (`removeDemoWhatsApp`), and unlinks any other customer someone linked to a demo lead or contact. The demo numbers are never the company's: `WhatsAppService.numbers()` leaves mode `demo` out, and the workspace's `whatsapp.configured` is `available()`, a connected number or a demo one.

The demo's companies are real, well-known Chinese companies (both languages, the English under their official English names): their names, cities, industries, rough sizes and real websites are public facts, while the people, deals and what happened with them are invented and kept neutral (no competitor chosen, budget cut, funding, incident or other claim that could read as a fact about the company; a lost or churned one built its own system instead). The contacts' email addresses stay at `<domain>.example`, never a real company's. Each lead's industry and city are `leadPlaces` in `demo.ts`. One project company, GAC, carries a real, neutral company signal with its real sources and date (`project.signals`; no model runs); removing the demo deletes its companies' signals. See [crm-signals.md](crm-signals.md).

LinkedIn addresses in the dataset: a company's is its real LinkedIn page (`DemoCompany.linkedin`) only where a search showed the page as that company's own, left out otherwise rather than guessed (Intime, Guangzhou Restaurant and XJTLU have none); a person's, since the people are made up, is `demoProfile(mailbox)` in `server/providers/crm/demo.ts`, `https://www.linkedin.com/in/nococrm-demo-<mailbox>`, an address no real profile has, as their email is at a `.example` domain: every primary contact and the other contact at every other company, the importer's projects' primary contacts, and the leads that came by exhibition, outreach or a partner.
